# Visual Style Bible

## Visual Target

The game should feel close in spirit to fresh, highly readable, highly animated 1990s cartoon adventure games, especially the playful freshness of Day of the Tentacle, without copying any single game.

The art must be:

- 2D hand-painted cartoon
- fresh
- elastic
- highly readable
- expressive
- silhouette-driven
- lively in perspective
- bold in outline
- rich in shape design
- exaggerated but clean

The art must not be:

- grim realistic painterly
- muddy
- stiff
- over-rendered
- flat corporate vector art
- anime
- cyberpunk
- generic mobile casual style

## Shape Language

- Use curved and slightly warped architecture.
- Use playful asymmetry.
- Exaggerate props based on story function.
- Make every important object readable by silhouette.
- Separate foreground, midground, and background clearly.
- Let environments look like reality filtered through gossip, poverty, and municipal neglect.
- Push perspective for comedy, but keep navigation readable.
- Avoid straight, sterile, CAD-like rooms unless the joke is bureaucratic stiffness.

Practical rule: if a prop has a joke, its shape should already hint at the joke before the player clicks it.

## Character Design

- Every main character must read from silhouette.
- Faces must be exaggerated, expressive, and funny.
- Use big noses, tired eyes, thick brows, clear mouth shapes, and asymmetrical details.
- Body shapes should communicate social role before dialogue starts.
- Avoid generic normal proportions for major NPCs.
- Avoid realistic celebrity likenesses.
- Avoid direct real politician likenesses.

### Bai Mitko

Bai Mitko must be instantly recognizable by:

- posture: slightly forward, survivor slouch, ready to explain himself
- moustache or strong facial hair detail
- tired face and alert eyes
- cheap clothing, likely tracksuit-influenced
- worn shoes
- accordion-player hands
- survivor energy: defeated by life, not by the current problem

He should look charming and questionable at the same time.

### NPC Shape Identities

- Baba Stoyanka: small but immovable triangle, shawl, sharp nose, compressed authority.
- Tony The Fridge: massive rectangular torso, tiny emotional center, heavy hands.
- Journalist: narrow vertical shape, alert head, camera/notebook silhouette.
- Mayor: inflated pear or poster-smile shape, soft hands, ceremonial posture.
- Clerk: desk-shaped person, hunched precision, stamp arm ready.
- Security Guard: wide belt, small rulebook energy, chest-first stance.
- Mehana Owner: round hospitality shape with calculating eyes.

## Color Direction

Core palette:

- dusty ochre
- socialist grey
- faded red
- dirty beige
- smoke yellow
- olive green
- muted teal accents
- old poster blues in moderation

Important rule:

Even though the world is shabby, the visuals must stay fresh and lively through strong value grouping, readable focal points, and enough color contrast.

Use color to guide gameplay:

- exits: slightly cooler or brighter value contrast
- interactables: local color pop or silhouette clarity
- danger/suspicion moments: faded red accents
- official bureaucracy: beige, grey, dull green, stamp red
- mehana warmth: smoke yellow, amber, deep brown, rakia highlights

Avoid:

- all-brown scenes
- all-grey scenes
- muddy low-contrast corners around important props
- neon cyberpunk accents
- glossy mobile-game saturation

## Background Rules By Chapter 1 Scene

### Bai Mitko's Apartment

Emotional mood:

- broke, intimate, absurdly self-important
- a private collapse trying to become a campaign office

Gameplay readability target:

- door exit must read immediately
- table, mirror, unpaid bills, accordion, TV, wardrobe, and campaign poster must be visually distinct
- lower floor must remain clear for walking

Visual density target:

- medium density
- clutter should frame the playable area, not obscure it

Prop comedy opportunities:

- lonely accordion displayed like a war medal
- unpaid bills stacked with tragic importance
- cracked mirror that makes Bai Mitko look more official by accident
- campaign poster draft with too much confidence for the room
- suspicious tracksuit hanging like a ceremonial uniform

Welcome distortion:

- peeling walls curling like tired paperwork
- table legs slightly warped
- TV leaning with judgment
- poster too proud for the apartment

### Village Square

Emotional mood:

- public theatre, dusty optimism, civic fatigue
- the village has seen every promise before and still gathers to watch

Gameplay readability target:

- hub exits must be clear: apartment, mehana, municipality
- NPC staging must leave readable click zones
- posters and election notice should invite Look interactions

Visual density target:

- medium-high density
- background can be rich, but major paths and NPCs must pop

Prop comedy opportunities:

- campaign posters pasted over older campaign posters
- broken fountain treated like a landmark
- statue of a forgotten local hero with confusing dignity
- old men bench as unofficial parliament
- public works sign promising "phase 3" of a repair nobody saw

Welcome distortion:

- pavement bends slightly toward the fountain
- posters stack like geological layers
- municipality entrance looks too important for its actual size
- kiosk leans like it knows secrets

### Mehana "The Two Goats"

Emotional mood:

- warm, smoky, dangerous hospitality
- unofficial parliament with kebapche smoke

Gameplay readability target:

- Tony's table must dominate the right side
- oil, water jug, receipt box, back door, grill, and radio must be readable
- lower walking area must stay open

Visual density target:

- high characterful density, but with strong value grouping
- clutter can be funny, not confusing

Prop comedy opportunities:

- framed goat with mayoral sash
- calendar from 2007 still somehow current
- rakia bottles arranged like local history
- receipt box that looks legally radioactive
- radio with more authority than the mayor

Welcome distortion:

- walls bowed by smoke and gossip
- Tony's table oversized like a throne
- grill glowing with civic danger
- bottles leaning toward the conversation

### Municipality Hall

Emotional mood:

- beige procedural purgatory
- the building is calm because it has already won

Gameplay readability target:

- stamp desk, archive cabinet, mayor door, ballot storage, ticket machine must be clear
- corridor depth should guide the eye to blocked authority

Visual density target:

- medium density with repetitive bureaucratic patterns
- visual comedy comes from labels, queues, forms, and useless order

Prop comedy opportunities:

- broken ticket machine still issuing emotional damage
- dusty plastic plants that look more employed than citizens
- portraits of past mayors with identical smiles
- stamp desk lit like a holy altar
- archive cabinet swollen with secrets and pickle smell

Welcome distortion:

- corridor slightly too long
- doors too tall or too narrow
- forms stacked like architecture
- portraits watching the player

### Election Booth / Final Scene

Emotional mood:

- democratic theatre under fluorescent exhaustion
- everyone knows the ritual matters and does not work cleanly

Gameplay readability target:

- ballot box, voters, journalist, mayor, workers, and exit must be immediately readable
- final choice/result staging must be visually clean

Visual density target:

- medium density
- leave space for ending animation, dialogue, and meter feedback

Prop comedy opportunities:

- transparent ballot box treated like sacred glass
- tired election workers with heroic resignation
- flickering lamp as civic suspense
- voters waiting like they are at a clinic
- mayor sweating through campaign confidence

Welcome distortion:

- folding tables slightly bowed by democracy
- fluorescent light too dramatic for the room
- ballot box visually larger than it should be

## UI Rules

UI must feel:

- retro adventure
- tactile
- slightly worn
- paper, stamp, folder, receipt, and campaign-poster influenced
- darkly comic
- highly readable

Use:

- stamped labels
- worn paper panels
- municipal red accents
- inventory slots like old desk trays or document folders
- clear verb buttons
- simple strong typography

Avoid:

- glossy modern app UI
- corporate clean blue panels
- generic RPG fantasy UI
- overly decorative frames
- tiny unreadable type

## Scene Comedy Layering

Every scene should contain:

- one main visual joke
- two secondary prop jokes
- background storytelling details
- readable interactables

Example structure:

- Main visual joke: the municipality stamp desk looks like an altar.
- Secondary prop joke: the ticket machine gives numbers for departments that do not exist.
- Secondary prop joke: plastic plant has an inventory tag.
- Background detail: old mayor portraits share the same smile.
- Readable interactables: stamp, archive cabinet, mayor door, ballot storage.

## Production Do/Don't Checklist

Do:

- check the scene reads at thumbnail size
- check the main silhouette before rendering detail
- keep walkable areas visually clear
- keep interactables separated by value or shape
- make props funny through shape and placement
- use Bulgarian signage where it adds authenticity
- preserve room for UI/dialogue overlays
- export foreground occluders separately when needed
- keep colors shabby but lively
- make every main NPC recognizable in silhouette

Don't:

- make scenes muddy because the world is poor
- over-render tiny props until the focal point disappears
- copy a specific LucasArts game's shapes directly
- use realistic politician faces
- place important objects only in dark corners
- bake characters into backgrounds
- make all architecture straight and stiff
- use generic mobile-game polish
- use flat corporate vector style
- approve art that cannot support readable gameplay
