# Humor Bible

## Humor Target

The humor in **Другарят Кандидат / Comrade Candidate** is:

- absurdist
- deadpan
- satirical
- visually playful
- character-driven
- bureaucratically surreal
- inspired by the spirit of 1990s cartoon adventure games such as Day of the Tentacle, Sam & Max, Full Throttle, and Monkey Island
- original and rooted in Bulgarian provincial political satire

This is not generic black comedy.

The premise can be dark, but the delivery must stay light, playful, and characterful. The game is cynical about systems, affectionate toward human weakness, and built from administrative absurdity, small corruption, village realism, and cartoon escalation.

## Core Joke Sources

Use these joke engines repeatedly:

- **Deadpan bureaucracy:** ridiculous actions described as normal administrative steps.
- **Verbal inflation:** tiny village problems get treated like historic national crises.
- **Character contradiction:** people say noble things while doing petty, selfish, or absurd things.
- **Visual exaggeration:** the environment makes jokes before anyone speaks.
- **Puzzle misdirection:** the obvious adventure-game solution is morally wrong, physically impossible, or administratively invalid.
- **Object comedy:** props have social history, political baggage, or suspicious paperwork attached.
- **Official language collapse:** formal phrases fail under village reality.
- **Civic theatre:** everyone knows the ritual is fake, but still performs it with pride.

## Dialogue Rules

- Keep lines short, quotable, and rhythmically sharp.
- Prefer one strong joke over three weak explanations.
- Characters should treat ridiculous situations as normal.
- Avoid long setup unless the payoff is very strong.
- Let Bai Mitko comment like a survivor, not a stand-up comedian.
- Make every NPC funny through worldview, not random punchlines.
- Do not use meme humor.
- Do not use random edginess.
- Do not write long heavy exposition.
- Do not explain the satire after the joke lands.

## Bulgarian And English

Bulgarian:

- must sound natural, local, sardonic, and idiomatic
- may use village phrasing, street-level political cynicism, bureaucratic absurdity, and dry resignation
- must not sound like literal translation from English
- should use recognizable Bulgarian rhythm without relying on obscure references

English:

- should preserve the comedic function, not exact wording
- should make the same kind of joke for international players
- may replace Bulgarian idioms with equivalent comic logic
- should not over-explain Bulgarian culture unless the joke needs it

## Tone Safety Rail

Do not use:

- graphic violence
- hateful caricature
- real politician depictions
- real party logos
- real-person accusations
- cruelty-first humor
- shock-only humor
- jokes based on ethnicity, disability, or protected identity

Satire should target:

- systems
- habits
- corruption
- vanity
- bureaucracy
- democratic theatre
- petty survival logic
- public cynicism
- small-town status games

The player should laugh at how people adapt to broken systems, not at people being dehumanized.

## Writing Rhythm Rules

- Most spoken lines should be 1 sentence.
- Strong dialogue lines should fit comfortably in a subtitle box.
- Use 2 short sentences when the second sentence reverses or sharpens the first.
- Avoid paragraphs except for rare official documents, speeches, or intentionally boring bureaucracy.
- Punchlines should appear often, but not every line needs to be a joke.
- Every conversation branch should contain at least one memorable line.
- Repetition works when the repeated phrase gets more absurd or more bureaucratic each time.
- Rule of three is useful: normal, worse, administratively impossible.
- Let silence, pauses, and blunt endings carry jokes.
- End scenes and puzzle results with clean button-like lines.

Example rhythm:

- Setup: "The form is missing."
- Escalation: "It needs a stamp proving it exists."
- Punch: "The stamp is in the department for imaginary property."

## Humor Examples By NPC

### Bai Mitko Petkov

- Treats shame as a renewable resource.
- Turns personal debt into public-sector vocabulary.
- Compares politics to accordion performance: squeeze, noise, applause, unpaid weddings.
- Claims incompetence is experience "from the other side."
- Uses moral disclaimers after already doing the thing.
- Finds administrative loopholes faster than honest work.
- Sounds tired, but never defeated.
- Speaks in practical survival logic, not ideology.
- Is weirdly charming because he knows exactly what he is.
- Makes small lies sound like municipal strategy.

### Baba Stoyanka

- Judges politics by whether it resembles known village habits.
- Accepts corruption if it is "traditional" and socially legible.
- Measures candidates against goats, winters, funerals, and road repairs.
- Says brutal things with grandmotherly calm.
- Treats every reform as something she has outlived before.
- Distrusts fancy words but respects ritual.
- Remembers every unpaid favor in the village.
- Uses proverbs that have become slightly weaponized.
- Is affectionate only after testing someone's usefulness.
- Believes the dead had better manners than current officials.

### Tony The Fridge

- Treats drinking as civic qualification.
- Respects strength, stamina, and emotional accordion music.
- Has simple rules that become dangerous puzzle constraints.
- Speaks like a refrigerator with opinions.
- Confuses intimidation with hospitality.
- Believes politics is just tavern dominance with paperwork.
- Gets sentimental fast, then denies it.
- Measures promises in bottles, not programs.
- Notices cheating only when it insults his personal code.
- Can become an ally if Bai Mitko cheats correctly, not disrespectfully.

### Journalist

- Asks simple questions that become traps.
- Wants truth but also needs a good headline.
- Treats Bai Mitko's evasions as a public service hazard.
- Uses calm professional phrasing over absurd accusations.
- Grows more dangerous when everyone else gets casual.
- Makes jokes by refusing to join the village's shared denial.
- Notices contradictions in posters, receipts, and body language.
- Can be funny through restraint.
- Should raise Suspicion when Bai Mitko improvises badly.
- Represents accountability without becoming heroic or preachy.

### The Mayor

- Speaks as if every failure was part of a long-term plan.
- Calls decay "phased modernization."
- Treats public property as inherited furniture.
- Uses slogans that almost mean something.
- Is vain about tiny achievements.
- Fears embarrassment more than justice.
- Smiles like a poster even when cornered.
- Blames weather, Europe, previous councils, and incorrect public expectations.
- Makes corruption sound boring, procedural, and unavoidable.
- Should be ridiculous, not a direct real-world copy.

### Clerk

- Believes paperwork is nature.
- Can block any action with the wrong stamp, missing stamp, old stamp, wet stamp, or stamp-related rumor.
- Treats citizens as badly formatted applications.
- Uses official language to avoid common sense.
- Is not evil; the desk has absorbed the soul.
- Can be distracted by protocol contradictions.
- Keeps absurd items in official places for "temporary categorization."
- Makes jokes through calm obstruction.
- Should sound tired, exact, and unbeatable.
- Represents bureaucracy as a puzzle monster.

### Security Guard

- Enforces rules he half-remembers.
- Takes his badge seriously because nothing else takes him seriously.
- Confuses suspicion with procedure.
- Gives warnings that sound improvised.
- Can be flattered by official-looking nonsense.
- Blocks doors for reasons invented after the fact.
- Is proud of preventing imaginary incidents.
- Responds to stamps, uniforms, and confident walking.
- Should be physically present but mentally negotiable.
- Provides low-stakes authority comedy.

### Mehana Owner

- Treats business, politics, and gossip as one accounting system.
- Knows everyone's debts.
- Has a receipt for things that should not have receipts.
- Speaks in customer-service warmth with threat underneath.
- Believes neutrality means serving all sides and charging twice.
- Provides items through favors, tabs, and suspicious storage.
- Comments on elections as if they are seasonal menu changes.
- Uses food metaphors for political failure.
- Should be practical, observant, and quietly dangerous.
- Makes the mehana feel like the village's unofficial parliament.

## Puzzle Humor Rules

- A puzzle solution should feel wrong in a funny, adventure-game way.
- The player should understand the comic logic after solving it.
- Avoid pure moon logic with no character or world justification.
- The best solution should reveal something about Bai Mitko or the NPC.
- Failure states should be funny and recoverable.
- Rewards should include a joke line, a state change, and sometimes a meter consequence.

## Approval Checklist For Writing

- Does the joke come from character, object, system, or situation?
- Is the line short enough to be memorable?
- Would the NPC naturally say it?
- Is the Bulgarian version idiomatic, not translated?
- Does the English version preserve the same comic job?
- Is the satire aimed at systems or habits instead of vulnerable people?
- Can the player continue quickly after the joke?
- Does the line avoid memes and random shock?

## Living Bulgarian Madness

- The game should feel aware of Bulgarian reality.
- Recent Bulgarian absurdity should feed flavor, not overwrite plot.
- Old Men Chorus, Journalist, TV, posters, and Municipality Hall are the best channels for current-feeling satire.
- Jokes should transform reality into fictional absurd mechanisms.
- Bulgarian should often be written first when the joke is culturally Bulgarian.
- English should adapt the joke, not translate it literally.
- Avoid topical jokes that become meaningless too quickly.
- Satire should age well.
- Use recent inspiration as optional flavor, not as a hard dependency in the main puzzle path.
- Do not use real politician names, direct real-person accusations, copied headlines, copied jokes, or copied social posts.
- If live browsing or recent-source access is unavailable, do not invent fake recent news; mark a TODO for a future satire intake pass and continue with evergreen Bulgarian bureaucratic satire.
